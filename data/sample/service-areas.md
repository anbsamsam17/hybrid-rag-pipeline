# Service Areas and Rest Stops

## Spacing of service areas

On a motorway, service areas with fuel, food and toilets appear roughly every 40 to 60 kilometres. Smaller rest areas with only parking and toilets are more frequent, so a driver is never far from a place to pause.

## Recommended break frequency

To fight tiredness, take a break of at least fifteen minutes every two hours of continuous driving. Fatigue is a factor in about one in five motorway crashes, so stopping to rest is not optional on a long trip.

## Warning signs of drowsiness

Drifting between lanes, heavy eyelids, yawning and missing an exit are early signals that you are too tired to continue. The only real cure is sleep; opening a window or turning up the radio does not restore alertness.

## Coffee and the nap trick

If you feel sleepy, the effective response is to drink a caffeinated drink and then nap for around twenty minutes while it takes effect. You wake as the caffeine kicks in, roughly doubling the short-term benefit.
