# Driving in Adverse Weather

## Fog and lights

In fog you must switch on dipped headlights; rear fog lights may be used only when visibility falls below 50 metres, and must be switched off once visibility improves so as not to dazzle others.

## Aquaplaning

Aquaplaning happens when a film of water lifts the tyres off the road and steering goes light. If it occurs, ease off the accelerator, do not brake hard, and hold the wheel straight until grip returns.

## High winds

Strong crosswinds are most dangerous for high-sided vehicles and when leaving the shelter of a cutting or tunnel. Grip the wheel firmly, reduce speed, and allow extra space around motorcycles and caravans.

## Black ice

Black ice is a thin transparent layer that is almost invisible on the road. It is most likely on bridges and in shaded spots at dawn; if the steering feels unusually quiet and light, suspect ice and slow down gently.
